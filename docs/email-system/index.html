Live emails

Pick an email, then switch language, width, theme and mail app. "Apple Mail" shows the interactive layer: the hamburger, the host cheering when tapped, the tips and the puzzles. "Gmail / Outlook" strips the checkboxes the way those clients do, so you see exactly what their readers get.

On the phone width, tap the ☰ in the header for the menu, and tap the friend at the top to make them cheer. Welcome 2 and "Brick of the week" hold a puzzle you can play.

The cast

Each email opens with one friend on a plinth in their own colour, saying one short line in the reader's language. The lines are written per mood and never quote a reader, so a forum post can never end up in a friend's mouth. The casting follows each friend's role in the game.

Foundations

Colours are the website's own tokens (app/styles/editorial.css), so an email and the page it links to feel like one place. Every text colour clears 4.5:1 on its surface in both themes. The puzzle bricks use the game's palette from DESIGN.md.

Body at 17 px with 1.6 line height; eyebrows at 13 px, 800, tracked uppercase. Japanese uses Hiragino Maru Gothic.

Frame

One 600 px column

Indigo header and footer, a cream reading plate, the six-colour course stripe above and below. Fluid on phones without media queries; Outlook gets a fixed ghost table.

Components

All in emails/core.ts and emails/friends.ts. Every interactive part degrades to readable, still content.

Host stage

The friend on a studded plinth with a speech bubble. In Apple Mail they hop in, breathe, wiggle on hover and cheer when tapped. Reduced motion switches it all off. With images off, the bubble is still real text and the image alt is the friend's name.

Header menu

In every reader email, as a ☰ at every width in Apple Mail, iOS Mail and Samsung Email; Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo strip the control a hamburger needs, so they show a plain row of links. Each email's checkbox ids are unique, so two OutBrick emails in a conversation never open each other's menu.

Studded button

The one action: a gold brick with a darker foot, two studs and a moulded highlight. Outlook on Windows draws the same shape in VML.

Puzzle board

A real OutBrick board in table cells: bricks, gates, studs. Tap the brick that moves first. The right one slides out through its gate, the rest follow, and the host cheers. A wrong one shakes and a hint appears. Other clients get the board, the answer and a link to the daily board.

Plates, bullets, breaks

Panels are plates with a row of studs; list bullets are small studded bricks in the course colours; section breaks are five little bricks in a row.

Brand footer

Social tiles, the App Store badge in the reader's language, copyright and the P.O. Box, plus unsubscribe and preferences where the email is marketing. The badge's campaign token names the email that was sent.

Analytics, end to end

Three layers, each with no raw address stored outside Resend and your own subscriber table.

In the email

  • Resend webhook stores every email.* event in email_events: sent, delivered, opened, clicked, bounced, complained, suppressed, failed.
  • Addresses are stored as a keyed hash; clicked links keep only path and utm_*.
  • Opens are marked approximate (Apple Mail Privacy Protection); clicks lead.

On the website

  • Every site link carries utm_source=outbrick-email, utm_medium=email and utm_campaign = the email's name, so Google Analytics credits each visit.
  • New GA4 events, behind the consent banner: generate_lead (newsletter sign-up), contact, sign_up (community).
  • Server milestones without cookies: sign-up, confirmed, unsubscribed, preferences saved.

In the App Store

  • The App Store badge in each email carries ct=email-<name>, so App Analytics can say which email sent a download.
  • Apple only counts it once the provider token is filled in (see To ship).

The report

  • New Email panel in /community/admin (admins only), 7 / 30 / 90 days.
  • Funnel: sign-ups → confirmed → welcome 2 → welcome 3 → still subscribed.
  • Per email: sent, delivered, clicks, opens, bounces, complaints, unsubscribes; by language; top links; release broadcasts.

What email can do next for retention

Brick of the week

A fortnightly puzzle issue, solved in the inbox, pointing to the daily board. Built and ready; it needs a puzzle and an issue file each time.

Link the game to an email

An opt-in "Get OutBrick news" in the app's Settings, sending the address with a hashed Game Center id. It unlocks milestone emails: a village finished, a chapter cleared, 100 days of play.

Gentle comeback

After 14 days away, one email from the friend whose village the player was in ("Moss kept your spot in Clover Farm"), with a puzzle. One per absence, never a streak guilt-trip.

Friend of the month

Poppy tells one friend's story, with a sticker and a themed board. Builds attachment to the cast the game is built around.

Seasonal events

The event ticket layout is ready for the winter festival: dates, what's new, a themed puzzle, hosted by Flurry.

Ask once, answer fast

The support loop already asks "did we solve it?" and reopens the case on "not yet". Add a yearly "What should we build next?" with one-tap choices to the newsletter.

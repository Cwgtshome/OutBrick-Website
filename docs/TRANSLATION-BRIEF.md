# Translation brief

Two kinds of translation live in this repository: the **string catalogs** that carry the site and
every email into the 33 languages added on 10–11 October 2026, and the **journal articles**, which are
translated into the first five languages only (French, German, Spanish, Japanese, Brazilian
Portuguese). The catalogs come first below.

## String catalogs (the 33 catalog languages)

The website and every email OutBrick sends are written in English, French, German, Spanish,
Japanese and Brazilian Portuguese inside the code. The other 33 languages of the game (it ko nl ru
tr zh-Hans ar ca cs da fi hr hu id ms nb pl ro sk sv th vi zh-Hant es-419 he pt-PT el uk fr-CA hi
sl ur bn) are **catalog languages**: their words live in flat JSON catalogs that map each English string
to its translation, and `lib/i18n/catalog.ts` applies them.

### Files

- `lib/i18n/catalog/en/<area>.json`: the English source, `{ "English": "English" }`. Generated by
  `scripts/i18n/extract.mts`; never edit it by hand.
- `lib/i18n/catalog/en/<area>.context.json`: where each string is used (a record and field name,
  a page phrase, a guide slug). Read it when a short string is ambiguous ("Play": a button, a
  verb; "Board": the game board).
- `lib/i18n/catalog/<locale>/<area>.json`: **your file**. The same keys, byte for byte, in the
  same order, with each value translated. Copy nothing else into it.
- `docs/i18n/glossary/<locale>.json`: the game's own words in your language, taken from the
  game's string catalog (`Brickout/Localizable.xcstrings`). `_samples` shows full sentences from
  the game: match their form of address and tone.

Areas: `emails`, `community` (forum interface), `ui` (site chrome, home, play guide, forms),
`support` (Support Centre), `help-ui` (Help Centre interface), `pages` (every public page's
phrases), `legal` (privacy policy, terms, licence, refunds, age rating, accessibility statement),
and eight `help-*` files (the Help Centre guides).

### How to translate

- Write natural, warm, family-friendly copy that a native speaker would take for original
  writing. Do not translate word for word, but keep every fact, number, limit and hedge exactly.
  OutBrick is a calm puzzle game for all ages: plain words, short sentences, no slang.
- **Game terms come from the glossary**, exactly: the Journey, the Brick Pass, the Move Booster
  Pass, lives, moves, coins, boosters, the Shop, Settings, every on-screen label. When the English
  puts a label in `**bold**`, it is the game's label: use the game's label in your language inside
  the same `**…**`. If the glossary lacks a term, choose the most natural established word and use
  it consistently.
- Names stay as they are: OutBrick, the nine friends (Bloo, Bricko, Flurry, Moss, Peach, Poppy,
  Sprout, Vio, Zippy), App Store, Game Center, iCloud, Apple, iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple TV, Apple
  Watch, Apple Vision Pro, Resend, Google. Apple features use Apple's official names in your
  language (VoiceOver, Switch Control, Voice Control, Reduce Motion, Dynamic Type …: check how
  iOS names them in your language).
- The friends never speak aloud in the game (no voices); speech bubbles are text. Do not add
  sound words.
- **Placeholders**: `{0}`, `{1}` … are filled in by the code. Keep every one, move them where your
  grammar needs them. When `{0}` is a count, you may choose plural forms:
  `{0, plural, one {# brick} few {# bricks} other {# bricks}}` (categories: zero, one, two, few,
  many, other as `Intl.PluralRules` defines them for your language; `#` is the number). Always
  include `other`. For languages without plural forms just write `{0} …`.
- **Markup**: `[words](target)` is a link: translate the words, keep the target exactly
  (`help:voiceover#setup`, `/support`, `https://…`). `{{Key}}` is a keyboard key or gesture: keep
  the braces, translate the key's name the way your keyboard labels it (`{{Esc}}` stays `{{Esc}}`
  where keyboards say Esc). Keep backticks around text to type. Keep `**` pairs balanced.
- **Length**: buttons, tabs, menu items and labels (short keys, see the context file) must stay
  short: aim for no more than about 1.5× the English. Prefer a shorter synonym to a long phrase.
- Typography and register by language:
  - Use the form of address the game uses in `_samples` (the informal `tu`, `du`, `sen` …
    where the game is informal, the polite form where it is polite).
  - French Canadian (`fr-CA`): Quebec usage and spelling (courriel, téléverser, magasiner), the
    game's French terms otherwise, vous; no space before `!` `?` `;`, a non-breaking space
    before `:` and inside « ».
  - Latin American Spanish (`es-419`): neutral Latin American Spanish, tú, ustedes (never
    vosotros), celular, computadora. Start from the game's Spanish glossary for game terms.
  - European Portuguese (`pt-PT`): ecrã, telemóvel, utilizador, ficheiro, transferir; tu or an
    impersonal form as the game's Brazilian copy uses você. Start from the pt-BR glossary for
    game terms and adapt to European usage.
  - Arabic, Hebrew and Urdu are right-to-left: write normally; keep Latin names (OutBrick, iPhone) in
    Latin script; keep digits as ASCII 0–9 in placeholders and numbers.
  - Chinese (`zh-Hans`, `zh-Hant`): full-width punctuation (，。：？！「」 for Traditional,
    “” for Simplified), a space between Chinese and Latin words or digits as the game does.
  - Japanese style is not needed here (Japanese is hand-written in code).
  - Thai: no spaces between words, a space between clauses.
  - Hindi (`hi`): Devanagari, आप, common English loanwords where Indian players use them (गेम, लेवल).
  - Urdu (`ur`): Nastaliq-friendly plain Urdu, آپ. Bengali (`bn`): standard Bengali, আপনি.
  - Hebrew (`he`), Greek (`el`), Ukrainian (`uk`), Slovenian (`sl`), Urdu (`ur`), Bengali (`bn`): the game is being translated
    now, so there is no glossary yet; choose natural, established gaming terms. Ukrainian must be
    idiomatic Ukrainian, never calqued from Russian.
- Legal text: translate faithfully and completely, in the plain register of the English. Keep
  every right, limit, date, address, email address and URL. Do not add or drop obligations.

### Checking

```sh
node --experimental-strip-types --no-warnings scripts/i18n/check.mts --locale <locale> --area <area>
```

It must report `errors: 0`. Read its warnings (long labels, strings left in English) and fix the
real ones. A large file may be written in parts (`<area>.part1.json`, `<area>.part2.json` …, each a
JSON object of some of the keys) and joined with `node scripts/i18n/merge-parts.mjs <locale> <area>`.

When English copy changes, `scripts/i18n/extract.mts` rewrites the English files; the check then
lists the keys each language is missing. A missing key falls back to English on the page, so a
change never breaks a page, but it should be translated before the next release.

## Translating journal articles

You translate one batch of 10 journal articles (`lib/journal-batches/batch-N.ts`) into one
language, into `lib/blog-l10n/extra/<locale>-<N>.ts` (it already exists and exports an empty
`ExtraGuides` object named `<locale><N>`, e.g. `fr3`). Touch no other file.

### Read first
- The top of `lib/i18n/blog.ts`: the `ExtraGuideTranslation` shape and `localizeArticle`, which
  fails the build if anything is missing.
- The existing cornerstone translations in your language, `lib/blog-l10n/<locale>.ts`. They set the house style:
  - register (vous / Sie / tú, as the home page uses it; です/ます in Japanese);
  - typography (French narrow no-break spaces before : ; ? ! and inside « »; Japanese 「」);
  - game terms as in `lib/i18n/home.ts` (French: plateau, brique, porte, annulation, objectif de coups…).
  Match them exactly.

### Each article
Key: the English slug. Value: an object with the fields below.
- `title`: phrased the way people search in that language. At most 60 characters, with CJK counted double.
- `dek`: at most 155 characters. It is the meta description.
- `imageAlt`: a translated description of the cover.
- `tags`: 4–6 terms people search for in that language.
- `intro`: the translated intro.
- `keyTakeaways`: the same number as the English.
- `sections`: keyed by the English section `id`. Each section has:
  - `title`;
  - `paragraphs`, the same count as the English;
  - `bullets`, the same count, only if the English has them;
  - `note`, only if the English has one.
- `pullQuote`: must appear word for word in your translated body.
- `faqs`: the same number as the English, and in the same order.

Write a natural, idiomatic article that a native reader would take for original writing. Do not translate word for word.
- Keep every fact, number and hedge exactly. Research findings must stay as cautious as in the
  English. No medical claims.
- Keep every inline link `[label](/path)`. Translate the label and keep the path exactly as in
  the English (`/blog/…`, `/play`, `/#fair`, `/daily`…). The site maps each path to your
  language's page when one exists. External links (`https://…`) stay as they are.
- In-text citations: keep the author names and years (e.g. "Sio et Ormerod (2009)").
  References are not translated and are not part of your file.
- Product and character names stay the same: OutBrick, Brick Pass, Bloo, Bricko, Flurry, Moss, Peach, Poppy, Sprout, Vio, Zippy.
  Use the App Store's official terms for Apple features in your language (VoiceOver, iCloud, widgets…).

### Check before you finish
`pnpm -s build` (localizeArticle throws on any count mismatch), `pnpm -s lint`,
`node scripts/audit-seo.mjs` (0 errors), `node scripts/check-links.mjs` (all resolve).
A guide is only published once all four languages carry it, so your pages may not render yet;
the build's shape checks still run on your file. Read your file once more against the English
for accuracy and tone, then commit on your branch. Do not push.

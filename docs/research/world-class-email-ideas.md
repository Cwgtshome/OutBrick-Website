# Link the game before sending more email

The companies that do email best do not send more of it; they tie each message to something the player actually did or declared (a streak, a race, a finished year, an in-game event) and stop when the messages stop working. The rigorous evidence for email's effect is modest and mostly from Duolingo's own experiments, where optimising reminder choice raised daily actives by **0.5%** and new-user retention by **2%** ([Yancey & Settles, KDD 2020](https://research.Duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)), while the eye-catching 300–900% lifts in circulation come from vendors selling the tooling. OutBrick's email system is already ahead of most small studios on craft, consent, support and hygiene. Its one structural gap is that **email knows nothing about play**: with no account and no link to Game Center progress, OutBrick cannot send the milestone, recap or "your village is waiting" mail that the best programmes are built on. It also cannot tell an active player who reads in Apple Mail from a dead address, because Apple-family clients now account for about **62% of tracked opens** and pre-load every pixel ([Webtonic summary of Litmus, July 2026](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/open-rate-statistics)). The recommendation is therefore two-track. First, spend the next few weeks on cheap website-side wins: separating transactional from marketing mail inside the 100-a-day Resend ceiling, registering with Apple's private relay, moving sunset rules off opens, fixing accessibility, completing consent records, and launching a monthly letter. Then build an optional, double-opt-in "connect your game" bridge that verifies the Game Center identity, which unlocks milestones, a Year in OutBrick and coordinated win-back. Throughout, keep to the calm, no-guilt and no-countdown rules that regulators are now writing into guidance.

## The best programmes trigger on what the player did, then stop

Across the companies with public detail, the shape repeats: an onboarding burst, triggered mail tied to real personal activity, an annual personal recap, and an explicit end when reminders fail. **Duolingo** keeps a pool of reminder templates personalised by course and streak, tests each new template on a small group, and demotes recently seen ones because novelty fades with repetition. Its analysis drew on about 200 million reminders over 34 days ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/hi-its-duo-the-ai-behind-the-meme/)). Its reactivation sequence ends with a "breakup" message after about a month of silence, which one columnist found respectful in effect and passive-aggressive in tone ([Debugger/Medium](https://debugger.medium.com/duolingo-needs-to-chill-8f1832745ca0)). **Strava's** strongest documented campaign starts from a race the runner *declared*, sends training content beforehand, and follows with a post-race email carrying the runner's own pace chart and a share button ([Iterable](https://iterable.com/blog/customer-spotlight-mikko-westberg-strava/)). **Supercell** emails update and event announcements, runs A/B tests on subject lines and layouts, and lets game teams own audience and copy. It reactivated players who had quit with a genuinely new collaboration (Clash of Clans × Chess.com), deep-linking installed players into the game and everyone else to the App Store ([Wudpecker webinar summary](https://www.wudpecker.io/blog/how-supercell-increases-user-loyalty-and-engagement-with-email-marketing-webinar-summary)). **Nintendo** emails a Switch Year in Review in some countries: games played, top three by hours, busiest months and recommendations ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/nintendo-switch-year-in-review-lets-you-see-how-much-time-you-spent-on-animal-crossing)).

Annual recaps show where email's role has shrunk. Duolingo's Year in Review **was an email newsletter in December 2019 and moved in-app with share cards from 2020** ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/year-in-review-behind-the-scenes)). Spotify Wrapped, the category's giant, reached **200 million engaged users in about 24 hours in 2025, with 500 million shares**, and all of that engagement is measured in-app ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-wrapped-campaign-hit-200m-engaged-users-in-24-hours-a-19-yoy-increase)). The growth comes from the share card, and Duolingo reports that adding an XP percentile and a "learner style" identity label raised share rates ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/year-in-review-behind-the-scenes)). Email's job in a recap is the announcement and the deep link.

**NYT Games** supplies the clearest newsletter model for a puzzle brand. Its weekly "Gameplay" opens with a named writer, recaps Wordle with a link to today's puzzle, sets a "Brain Tickler" with the answer at the bottom, nudges readers into the day's Spelling Bee forum, reposts player posts as "Community Chatter", and offers an "Easy Mode" sign-up for an easier weekly crossword ([The Audiencers](https://theaudiencers.com/wordle-and-beyond-how-games-are-impacting-publisher-newsletters/)). **Linear's** changelog, written to show early adopters how fast fixes landed, explains *why* as well as *what*. It runs at about 2.8 entries a month, each with one hero image and a benefit-led headline ([Linear blog](https://linear.app/blog/startups-write-changelogs); [Changebot](https://changebot.ai/blog/how-linear-uses-product-updates-to-create-yell-from-the-rooftop-fans/)). Among indies, **Failbetter Games** sent one personal, plain-text, single-ask email to "hundreds of thousands" of opted-in Fallen London players. It drew **26% opens, 2% clicks, 0.6% unsubscribes and about 1,200 Steam wishlists**, which its communications director said matched a major Steam event. Their advice was to reintroduce yourself, write one-to-one in the house voice, and make one ask ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/06/28/using-mailing-lists-to-gain-thousands-of-wishlists/)). **Puzzmo** used its newsletter as a five-minute head start before daily invite keys dropped. The scarcity launch drew **104,000 players making 932,000 attempts** with zero ad spend ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/zach-gage-is-letting-500-people-into-his-new-puzzle-app-each-day-but-theres-a-catch); [INMA](https://inma.org/blogs/ideas/post.cfm/hearst-cracks-the-engagement-code-with-puzzmo)). Zach Gage also caps daily play deliberately to avoid burnout ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2024/08/interview-game-developer-zach-gage-on-pile-up-poker-and-resisting-dark-patterns/)).

The wellness apps are a cautionary contrast for a calm game. A 2020 teardown of **Calm** counted 10 emails in about 135 days: a 40%-off discount on day 0 and again on day 10, and an upgrade call to action on every later themed email ([Growth Snippets](https://growthsnippets.substack.com/p/every-email-calm-sent-me-after-i)). The only **Headspace** source found is a term-start student discount email ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/?p=288693)). That is a subscription-funnel pattern, and an ad-supported game with optional purchases has little reason to copy it.

The research found **no sourced detail** on the email programmes of King/Candy Crush, Airbnb, Notion, Apple's own marketing, Headspace's onboarding sequence, or calm indie games such as Monument Valley, Alto's Odyssey and Stardew Valley. Those studios appear to talk to players mainly in-game, in store pages and on social media, but that is unverified. Any claim that "Airbnb does X" or "King does Y" in email should be treated as unsupported. Apple's relevance here is as the rule-setter: Mail Privacy Protection, App Review and BIMI shape what everyone else can do.

## Measured lifts are single digits; vendor lifts are ten times larger

The honest effect sizes are small relative gains on very large bases. Beyond the KDD bandit result, Duolingo reports that allowing two Streak Freezes raised daily active learners by **0.38%**, and that separating the daily goal from the streak raised D14 retention by **3.3%** ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). Most of that work concerns push and product mechanics rather than email, and Duolingo publishes no email-only figure. The one games holdout on email itself is old. Kabam told Casual Connect in 2012 that emailed players retained at 7% against 5% for a no-email holdout ([Adrian Crook liveblog](https://adriancrook.com/email-marketing-tactics-to-drive-player-engagement-and-retention/)). Duolingo credits "new emails and notifications" in its resurrection work, where resurrected users rose from 5% to 8% of DAU alongside a Japanese-course newsletter. That is a correlation, not a test ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/back-from-the-brink-what-duolingo-learned-about-its-resurrected-users)).

Set those against the vendor numbers. AMP sellers report a 360% conversion uplift for Kotak and a 900% CTOR rise for MakeMyTrip ([Netcore](https://netcorecloud.com/?p=101511); [Stripo](https://stripo.email/blog/amp-emails-fresh-statistics-or-how-companies-earn-with-amp-for-email/)). Pushwoosh claims **15% of uninstalled players return** via win-back emails, without method ([Pushwoosh](https://www.pushwoosh.com/products/email-marketing-gaming/)). Win-back recovery of 5–15% is a recycled vendor range ([Mailsoftly](https://mailsoftly.com/blog/re-engagement-email-campaign/)). The claim that preference centres cut unsubscribes by 20–30% circulates between vendors with no traceable study ([Sender](https://www.sender.net/blog/email-preference-center/)). Braze's cross-channel report even contradicts itself on retention uplift ([Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/reports-and-guides/cross-channel-messaging)). The more believable gamification result is an agency's Kärcher roulette-wheel email, which lifted CTR from **2.9% to 6.6%**. Even its authors say it works only with a genuine reason to click ([Promodo](https://www.promodo.com/case-studies/how-to-increase-ctr-through-email-gamification)).

Measurement is the second reason to distrust headline numbers. Mail Privacy Protection pre-fetches pixels, so an Apple Mail open is no longer a human act. Brevo's entertainment benchmark is **21.26% excluding MPP but 37.45% including it** ([Brevo](https://www.brevo.com/blog/email-marketing-benchmarks/)). Published games open rates span 18% to 57% depending on vendor and definition ([MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/compare-your-email-performance-metrics-industry-benchmarks); [Designmodo](https://designmodo.com/email-marketing-benchmarks-by-industry/)). Clicks survive MPP, but they can be inflated by security scanners ([University of Colorado eComm wiki](https://www.cu.edu/blog/ecomm-wiki/artificial-email-engagement-rates)). A rule such as "suppress after 90 days without an open" behaves unpredictably under MPP ([Suped](https://www.suped.com/knowledge/email-deliverability/technical/how-does-apple-mail-privacy-protection-mpp-affect-the-tracking-of-email-opens-and-clicks-and-how)). For OutBrick, whose players are by definition iPhone owners, open rate is close to meaningless. The usable operating guardrails are clicks of roughly 2–5%, unsubscribes under 0.2–0.5% per send ([ActiveCampaign](https://www.activecampaign.com/glossary/email-marketing-benchmarks)) and complaints under **0.10%**, never reaching 0.30% ([Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). The only effect size worth believing will come from OutBrick's own random holdout of 10–20% of recipients on each programme.

What the evidence does support is restraint and variety. Duolingo's gains came from *choosing better* among messages, and a secondhand account says raising notification *volume* needed strong justification and CEO approval ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). Frequency is the top reason people unsubscribe in vendor surveys ([Mailgun](https://www.mailgun.com/blog/email/understanding-unsubscribes/)). Since July 2025, Gmail's "Manage subscriptions" view lists senders by how often they write and unsubscribes in one tap ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/07/manage-email-subscriptions-in-gmail.html)). The all-industry unsubscribe rate more than doubled in 2025, to 0.22% ([MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/compare-your-email-performance-metrics-industry-benchmarks)). A low, predictable cadence is now itself a retention feature.

## OutBrick has the plumbing; it lacks the link to play

Measured against the companies above, OutBrick's system is unusually complete for a small studio. It has friend-hosted, localized templates; a welcome series; content-led release emails; support threads with case IDs and "fixed in X" follow-ups; a 120-day re-permission with sunset; topics; consent records; bounce and complaint sync; and privacy-preserving analytics. The 120-day window matches one practitioner definition of an engaged subscriber as one with a click in 120 days ([Mailflow Authority](https://mailflowauthority.com/list-hygiene/sunset-policies-guide)). The interactive puzzle emails target the right client: CSS "kinetic" email works in Apple Mail, while AMP reaches only Gmail, Yahoo and Mail.ru and needs per-sender allowlisting ([FreshInbox](https://freshinbox.com/blog/the-dawn-of-kinetic-email/); [amp.dev](https://amp.dev/documentation/guides-and-tutorials/start/email_sender_distribution)).

Four constraints shape what comes next. The first is that **email is blind to play**. Every high-value trigger in the research needs first-party behavioural data: Strava's race, Duolingo's streak, Nintendo's hours, a percentile for the share card. OutBrick has rich progress data (2,000 boards, 167 villages, nine friends) on the device, but nothing joins it to a confirmed address. The robust way to join them without accounts is server-verified Game Center identity. The app calls `fetchItems(forIdentityVerificationSignature:)`, the server checks Apple's signature over `teamPlayerID`, bundle ID, timestamp and salt, and the server binds that ID to an address confirmed by a single-use magic link ([Apple GameKit docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/gamekit/gklocalplayer/fetchitems(foridentityverificationsignature:)); [Metaplay](https://docs.metaplay.io/feature-cookbooks/social-logins/social-platform-apple-game-center.html)). `teamPlayerID` can be unavailable on some accounts, so an install UUID kept in Keychain or iCloud KVS is the fallback ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/683668)). The return Universal Link must be handled in `SceneDelegate`, because the project's own notes record that the `UIApplicationDelegate` entry points no longer run. The App Store privacy label must then declare Email Address and User ID as "Linked to You" for marketing; hashing the address does not remove the disclosure ([Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)).

The second constraint is that **the sunset signal is weak** without that link. Today the only honest engagement evidence is a click or a website visit. A loyal player who reads every letter in Apple Mail and then opens the game from the Home Screen looks dead to the 120-day rule. The bridge fixes this too, because an app session is an engagement signal MPP cannot fake.

The third is **quota**. A free Resend plan at 100 emails a day means a single release email to a list of a few thousand takes weeks. Every marketing send also competes with support replies, security notices and forum notifications for the same daily budget. That ceiling, not the Gmail 5,000-a-day bulk threshold, is the binding limit. Staged sending is good practice anyway: Spamhaus warns that mailing all inactive users at once destroys reputation ([Spamhaus](https://www.spamhaus.org/resource-hub/deliverability/what-is-an-email-sunset-policy-and-why-do-you-need-one/)). But support and security mail must never wait behind a newsletter.

The fourth is that **the app already owns the short-cycle nudges**. Per `docs/retention.md`, local notifications already cover stuck boards, full lives, chapters, 3- and 7-day comebacks, the streak, events, season endings and a Sunday weekly recap. They are capped at one per 20 hours with quiet nights, and the same document explicitly rejects guilt-trip escalation and any notification that opens on the shop. Email should not duplicate that layer. Its distinct jobs are the ones the channel research assigns it: rich, non-urgent content (letters, milestones, launches) and win-back after the app's own nudges have gone quiet ([OneSignal](https://documentation.onesignal.com/docs/channel-setup); [Customer.io](https://docs.customer.io/journeys/message-channels)). The Braze model, a single per-user daily cap across push and email in the user's time zone, is the one to mirror once both channels can see each other ([Braze docs](https://www.braze.com/docs/user_guide/messaging/messaging_fundamentals/frequency_capping)).

## Mailbox providers, regulators and Apple now enforce trust

Deliverability rules have moved from advice to rejection. Gmail requires every sender to have SPF or DKIM, valid reverse DNS, TLS and a spam rate under 0.3%. Bulk senders also need aligned DMARC and RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe plus a visible body link ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)). Vendors report Gmail moving to temporary and permanent rejections of non-compliant bulk mail in November 2025 ([Valimail](https://www.valimail.com/?p=11424)). Yahoo requires unsubscribes honoured within two days ([Yahoo Sender Hub](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)), and Microsoft applied the same core rules to Outlook.com from May 2025 ([dmarcian](https://dmarcian.com/?p=20678)). Google also asks for a consistent From address per category and says promotions should not be mixed into receipts. Postmark separates transactional and broadcast streams for the same reason: shared infrastructure gets time-sensitive mail treated as bulk ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126); [Postmark](https://postmarkapp.com/support/article/can-i-send-bulk-emails)). Because the website already offers Sign in with Apple, one item is urgent and easy to miss. Mail to `@privaterelay.appleid.com` addresses **bounces unless every sending domain and subdomain is registered** with Apple's Private Email Relay service with aligned SPF or DKIM ([Apple Developer](https://developer.apple.com/help/account/capabilities/configure-private-email-relay-service/)). BIMI logos need DMARC at quarantine or reject. A VMC, which needs a registered trademark, is reportedly required for Apple Mail. Certificates cost roughly **US$650–1,700 a year**, and no independent study shows an effect on opens ([Google Workspace](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/security/set-up-bimi); [CaptainDNS](https://www.captaindns.com/en/blog/bimi-vmc-cmc-certificate-guide)).

On consent, one global design satisfies every market OutBrick mails. That means an unticked opt-in separate from any terms, double opt-in, and a stored record of the exact wording, form version, source and confirmation. The Bavarian data protection authority's 2025 report says a date, IP address and timestamp alone do not prove informed consent ([2B Advice](https://2b-advice.com/en/2026/03/31/proof-of-effective-consent-for-direct-advertising/)). German case law makes double opt-in the de facto standard ([Suped](https://www.suped.com/learn/email-deliverability/which-countries-require-double-opt-in-for-email-marketing-according-to-gdpr-and-best-practices)). CAN-SPAM requires a postal address and identification as an advertisement in commercial mail ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). Japan's opt-in law requires the sender's name, address and an opt-out address in each advertising email ([Japanese Law Translation](https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/laws/view/3767/en)). Brazil's LGPD rejects pre-ticked boxes ([Hosaki Law](https://hosakilaw.com/en/posts/lgpd-email-marketing-legal-basis-brazil/)). A puzzle game with friendly characters must assume some young players. Under the amended COPPA Rule, fully enforceable since **22 April 2026**, an email address from an under-13 needs verifiable parental consent ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)). The UK Children's Code defaults marketing opt-ins to off for under-18s ([MFMac](https://www.mfmac.com/insights/data-protection/ico-issues-advice-to-games-designers-to-comply-with-the-children-s-code/)). A neutral age gate before any sign-up form is the usual answer.

Apple's rules for in-app capture are permissive but sharp at the edges. Guideline 5.1.1 allows asking for an email only if it is optional and no feature depends on it. Guideline 5.1.2(i) bans rewarding the enabling of system features, which includes push. Guideline 4.5.4 requires a separate in-app opt-in and an off switch for marketing push ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). No guideline mentions rewards for newsletter sign-ups. June's Journey grants a one-time gift only after double opt-in ([Wooga help centre](https://wooga.theymes.com/hc/en/junes-journey/articles/how-to-sign-up-for-junes-journey-newsletter-521)), but that is evidence of practice, not of policy. A confirmed email list used first-party is not ATT "tracking". **Matching it against an ad network's audiences would be** ([Apple App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)), and that line matters for an AdMob-funded game.

The pressure tactics that work in Duolingo's notifications are the ones regulators now name. The FTC's 2022 dark-patterns report lists "nagging" and "confirmshaming" ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/blogs/2022/09/the-ftc-brings-more-light-to-dark-patterns-in-new)). The EU consumer-protection network's 2025 principles on in-game currencies object to time-limited pressure and direct appeals to children, and its coordinated action named King and Supercell ([Gleiss Lutz](https://www.gleisslutz.com/en/know-how/new-guidelines-game-currencies-digital-consumer-protection-and-expanding-taboo-dark-patterns); [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/video-games-europe-hits-back-at-eu-watchdog-targeting-king-supercell-mojang-and-more-over-iap-practices/)). No regulator has yet acted on lifecycle email specifically. But an email that sells with a countdown or threatens a lost streak falls squarely into the named categories, and Business Insider's reporting shows the user cost of guilt even when the metrics look fine ([Business Insider NL](https://businessinsider.nl/duolingo-is-mean-whiny-and-annoying-gen-z-loves-it/)).

Accessibility and localization are cheap places to be best in class. The Email Markup Consortium found that **99.89% of 443,585 commercial emails** had serious or critical accessibility failures. The commonest are missing `lang`/`dir`, layout tables without `role="presentation"`, no `h1`, and links without text. Gmail supports neither `prefers-color-scheme` nor `prefers-reduced-motion` ([EMC 2025](https://emailmarkup.org/en/reports/accessibility/2025/)). The kinetic checkbox technique behind in-email puzzles can be made only "a little less inaccessible" to screen readers ([Mark Robbins](https://noti.st/markrobbins/bjF6KX/slides)). It has no persistent state, and forwarded copies let someone else vote ([Ted Goas](https://dev.to/tedgoas/my-first-interactive-email-94)). For an app with active VoiceOver players, the static version must be the real content and the interaction the decoration. Localization guidance is vendor opinion, but consistent: formal register and longer subject lines in German, low-emoji formality in Japanese (where LINE takes some of email's role), and warmer copy in Brazilian Portuguese ([Omnisend](https://www.omnisend.com/blog/email-localization/); [Stripo](https://stripo.email/amp-version/webinars/beyond-translation-what-global-brands-get-wrong-about-email-marketing-in-east-asia)).

On support, OutBrick already matches the strongest documented practice: a case ID, a reply-to thread, a resolution check and a proactive "fixed in" note. The refinements in the research are specific. Zendesk sends its CSAT email **about 24 hours after solve**, so the player can confirm the fix first ([Macha](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/zendesk-csat-explained)). Other tools survey a case at most once ever ([Chatbase](https://www.chatbase.co/docs/user-guides/chatbot/help-desk/satisfaction-surveys)). Email surveys get about 6% response against 16% for iOS in-app ([Kustomer, citing SurveyPal](https://staging4.kustomer.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Kustomer-Guide_CSAT-Ratings.pdf)), so a score should always be reported with its response rate. Email first response averages about 6 h 47 min in calendar hours ([Freshworks](https://www.freshworks.com/freshdesk-benchmark-report-2023-automation-ai-cx-blog)), so "within one working day" is an honest promise. Supercell asks for recovery codes only inside in-game tickets, a channel rule built against phishing ([Supercell support](https://support.supercell.com/clash-royale/en/articles/ive-lost-access-to-my-account.html)). Magic links need a confirm click, because mail scanners consume single-use tokens ([Nhost](https://nhost.io/blog/protect-magic-links-from-email-clients)).

## A two-track roadmap: website wins now, the game bridge next

Effort is S (days, website or backend only), M (one to three weeks), or L (app release plus backend). Evidence is **Strong** (primary policy, law, peer-reviewed or company-measured data), **Moderate** (documented practice by a named company, no outcome numbers) or **Weak** (vendor claim or single anecdote). Items are in priority order within each track.

### What not to do

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Write guilt, sadness or "breakup" copy ("Bloo is sad", "these reminders aren't working") | Read as passive-aggressive even when it stops mail ([Debugger](https://debugger.medium.com/duolingo-needs-to-chill-8f1832745ca0)); already on OutBrick's skip list in `docs/retention.md` |
| Put countdowns, "last chance" or streak-loss threats in email, especially on weekly deals | Time-limited pressure and nagging are named by the EU CPC network and the FTC ([Gleiss Lutz](https://www.gleisslutz.com/en/know-how/new-guidelines-game-currencies-digital-consumer-protection-and-expanding-taboo-dark-patterns); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/blogs/2022/09/the-ftc-brings-more-light-to-dark-patterns-in-new)) |
| Reward push opt-in, or offer an email reward in the same prompt as the push permission | Guideline 5.1.2(i) ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) |
| Make email a condition of any feature, gift or progress | Guideline 5.1.1 requires it to be optional ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) |
| Upload addresses or hashed addresses to AdMob or any ad network audience | That becomes ATT "tracking" and a privacy-label change ([Apple App Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)) |
| Enrol under-13s without verifiable parental consent, or default UK minors into marketing | COPPA amended Rule; UK Children's Code ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect); [MFMac](https://www.mfmac.com/insights/data-protection/ico-issues-advice-to-games-designers-to-comply-with-the-children-s-code/)) |
| Sunset or segment on opens | MPP fabricates them ([Brevo](https://www.brevo.com/blog/email-marketing-benchmarks/)) |
| Mail every inactive address at once, or quietly re-add suppressed ones | Reputation damage and invalid consent ([Spamhaus](https://www.spamhaus.org/resource-hub/deliverability/what-is-an-email-sunset-policy-and-why-do-you-need-one/); [Omnisend](https://support.omnisend.com/en/articles/3405334-what-is-an-email-sunset-policy)) |
| Add promotions to support, security or receipt mail | Google's category rules; it also pulls those messages under marketing law ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)) |
| Reward or prompt reviews by email | Apple acts against incentivized feedback ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)); OutBrick's rating rules already forbid it |
| Forecast results from vendor lift figures (AMP 360–900%, 15% win-back, 20–30% fewer unsubscribes) | No method behind them; measure with your own holdout |

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which emails should OutBrick add?" to "what does OutBrick's email need to *know*?" The craft, consent and support layers are already near the top of what any studio publishes. The programmes that make Duolingo, Strava and Nintendo email feel personal all depend on a data link OutBrick does not have, and the same missing link makes the sunset rule blind to its most loyal readers. The Game Center–verified bridge is therefore not a feature among others. It is the prerequisite that turns half the roadmap from guesswork into something measurable. The Year in OutBrick is the one personal programme that can ship before it, because the recap lives on the device and email only has to announce it.

The other implication is that OutBrick's calm positioning is a competitive asset in email, not a handicap. The measured gains from optimising messages are a few percent. The costs of over-sending have become structural: one-tap unsubscribe lists sorted by frequency, doubled unsubscribe rates, rejection-level authentication rules, and regulators naming nagging and countdowns. A low-cadence letter in a friend's voice, mail that stops cleanly, and a holdout on every programme are both kinder and more likely to survive the next round of rules than a louder programme. They also produce the one thing the public evidence cannot supply: OutBrick's own effect sizes.

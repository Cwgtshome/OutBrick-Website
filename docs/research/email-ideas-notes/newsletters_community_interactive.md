# Newsletters, community email, referral programs and interactive email

Research date: 9 October 2026. About 22 searches and fetches. Most of the hard numbers in this area come from vendors, which have a commercial interest, or from secondhand write-ups. Each finding says which. Where I found no primary source, the item is listed under Gaps rather than stated as fact.

## Newsletter craft: how admired brands structure, pace and voice their newsletters

### Takeaway
The best-documented games newsletter, NYT Games' weekly "Gameplay", follows a repeatable pattern: a letter from a named writer, a recap of the daily game, a puzzle in the email with its answer at the bottom, a nudge toward the community, and a "community chatter" round-up of player posts. Product-update emails that people value (Linear, PostHog) are short, lead with a visual and explain why something changed, not just what. Open rates are no longer a usable benchmark, because Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates them. Clicks and outcomes are the metrics to track.

### Cited Findings
- **NYT Games "Gameplay" newsletter (observed December 2023):** sent weekly in the morning. The observed order was: a writer's introduction; a teaser for a longer story; a weekly Wordle recap linking to that day's puzzle; a "Brain Tickler" puzzle with the answer at the bottom; a house ad; a Spelling Bee puzzle with a link to play and a nudge to join that day's forum; one crossword clue; an ad; "Community Chatter" (screenshots from Twitter, comments and other community spaces); "Here's More From Us"; and a footer with the Brain Tickler answer and a sign-up for "Easy Mode", a newsletter offering an easier version of a hard weekly crossword. — [The Audiencers, 4 Dec 2023](https://theaudiencers.com/wordle-and-beyond-how-games-are-impacting-publisher-newsletters/)
- NYT games also appear inside the flagship newsletter The Morning (Spelling Bee, the news quiz and the Mini linked). The same article cites NiemanLab for The Morning having more than 17 million subscribers. — [The Audiencers](https://theaudiencers.com/wordle-and-beyond-how-games-are-impacting-publisher-newsletters/)
- NYT Games scale in 2022: Wordle played more than 2 billion times, all NYT Games almost 4 billion times, the Mini more than 500 million times. Per Axios, quoting NYT Games executive producer Zoe Bell, about 35% of Games subscribers cite Wordle as their reason for subscribing. The article argues that a Wordle → Gameplay newsletter → other games loop drives retention, but presents no data for it. — [The Audiencers](https://theaudiencers.com/wordle-and-beyond-how-games-are-impacting-publisher-newsletters/)
- "Wordplay" is the NYT crossword column (senior editor Deb Amlen), not a confirmed standalone newsletter. — [Notre Dame "With a Side of Pod"](https://withasideofpod.nd.edu/episodes/4-2-on-crosswords-and-wordplay-deb-amlen-the-new-york-times/)
- Washington Post "Game Break" newsletter: the crossword, the "On the Record" news quiz and Keyword, a new game. — [The Audiencers](https://theaudiencers.com/wordle-and-beyond-how-games-are-impacting-publisher-newsletters/)
- **Linear changelog (company blog, 2020):** written from the start to show early adopters how fast fixes landed. "Over a thousand people" followed it and forwarded it to non-users. Most job candidates had followed it before applying. The team treats releases as a shared celebration. — [Linear blog, "Startups write changelogs"](https://linear.app/blog/startups-write-changelogs)
- Third-party analysis of Linear's changelog: public by default; 192 entries at about 2.8 a month, usually 4–14 days apart; explains *why* as well as *what*. Releasepad describes each entry as one hero image, a headline about the user's benefit and a short paragraph. — [Changebot](https://changebot.ai/blog/how-linear-uses-product-updates-to-create-yell-from-the-rooftop-fans/); [Releasepad](https://www.releasepad.io/blog/release-notes-examples-how-10-top-saas-companies-communicate-product-updates/)
- PostHog's monthly "What's new" email is cited as an effective changelog email because it leads with visuals, keeps each feature to a short self-contained story and uses light calls to action. — [Swipefile](https://swipefile.com/why-posthogs-changelog-emails-hit-so-hard)
- **Benchmarks:** Mailchimp's industry averages (2015 data, quoted secondhand) were a 22.3% unique open rate and 2.8% click rate overall; its "Arts and Artists" row was 27.86% open and 2.98% click. No games row was found. — [SmartBug Media](https://www.smartbugmedia.com/blog/every-email-marketing-benchmark-you-will-ever-need)
- Litmus says Apple Mail Privacy Protection now affects roughly 55–60% of all email opens (page undated). Benchmark Email, citing Litmus' May 2025 data, says more than 50%. Apple Mail and Gmail together hold nearly 90% of the email client market. Recommended metrics are clicks, conversions and replies. — [Litmus](https://www.litmus.com/email-client-market-share); [Benchmark Email, June 2025](https://www.benchmarkemail.com/blog/mail-privacy-protection)
- MailerLite notes that MPP auto-marks Apple Mail messages as opened, so open rates from before 2021 cannot be compared with current ones. — [MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/blog/compare-your-email-performance-metrics-industry-benchmarks)
- **Duolingo reminders (peer-reviewed, KDD 2020):** a "sleeping, recovering" bandit that picks reminder copy, built to handle novelty effects and the need to vary messages, raised total DAU by 0.5% and new-user retention by 2% over a strong baseline. A secondhand account says Duolingo could not raise notification *volume* without strong justification and CEO approval. — [Yancey & Settles, KDD '20 (PDF)](https://research.Duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf); [Vicki Boykis commentary](https://vicki.substack.com/p/duo-the-push-and-the-bandits)
- A claim that Duolingo's guilt-based messages raised retention by 3% appears only in a secondary article, with no primary source. — [Gigazine, Jan 2025](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250114-how-duolingo-successful-business)

### Inferences
- The Gameplay pattern of named writer, puzzle in the email, answer at the bottom, community round-up and an easier "mode" for newcomers carries over directly to a puzzle game's weekly letter. Putting the answer at the bottom gives readers a reason to scroll and to come back.
- An "Easy Mode" sign-up shows that splitting a list by skill level is accepted practice. A games newsletter could split its list by player progress in the same way.
- Duolingo's evidence says that *varying* message content matters, and that the gains from optimising copy are real but small (around 0.5–2%). Restraint on volume, guarded at CEO level, is the stronger signal.
- Because MPP inflates opens, any benchmark of 40%+ open rates for a games newsletter should be treated with suspicion. A plan should set click and return-to-app targets instead.

### Gaps
- No primary or recent data found for the newsletters of Morning Brew, The Hustle, Notion, Figma, Airbnb, Apple, Patagonia, Headspace or Nintendo: cadence, open or click rates, segment lists. Not researched in depth because of the tool-call budget.
- No current (2024–2026) open or click benchmark for the games or entertainment category was found from Mailchimp, Klaviyo, Beehiiv or Substack.
- Gameplay's subscriber count and launch date were not found. Whether NYT runs separate companion emails for Connections or Spelling Bee is unconfirmed.

## Puzzle and game content delivered by email

### Takeaway
Puzzles in newsletters are well established: NYT's Brain Tickler with its answer in the footer, Spelling Bee links and community forums. Puzzmo used its newsletter as an early-warning channel tied to scarcity. Measured engagement for *email-delivered* puzzles specifically is almost never published.

### Cited Findings
- NYT Gameplay puts a puzzle in the body and its answer at the bottom, and nudges readers to "join today's forum" for Spelling Bee. — [The Audiencers](https://theaudiencers.com/wordle-and-beyond-how-games-are-impacting-publisher-newsletters/)
- **Puzzmo (Hearst, Zach Gage), 2023 beta:** the newsletter gave subscribers about five minutes' warning before the daily invite keys dropped. The first 500 people each day to complete the daily puzzle were mailed a key. — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/zach-gage-is-letting-500-people-into-his-new-puzzle-app-each-day-but-theres-a-catch)
- INMA's case study: the scarcity launch drew 104,000 players making 932,000 attempts to get in, with zero ad spend. Admitted players could give keys to two friends. Free daily puzzles accounted for "nearly an hour" of play a day. — [INMA](https://inma.org/blogs/ideas/post.cfm/hearst-cracks-the-engagement-code-with-puzzmo)
- Gage deliberately caps play, for example five hands of Pile-Up Poker a day, to avoid burnout. — [Six Colors, Aug 2024](https://sixcolors.com/post/2024/08/interview-game-developer-zach-gage-on-pile-up-poker-and-resisting-dark-patterns/)
- Puzzmo Plus (about $40 a year) adds leaderboards, archives and experimental games. — [Postmedia/NMC](https://nmc-mic.ca/2024/04/04/postmedia-launches-puzzmo-an-online-puzzle-games-experience-in-canada/)
- **Stripo single-game test:** an email asking readers to click their favourite item and then revealing a "prediction" got twice the clicks of a control without the game. Vendor-reported. — [GetResponse/Stripo roundup via search](https://www.getresponse.com/blog/email-gamification)

### Inferences
- An email does not need to *host* the game. Its job is to give a reason to open the app today: an answer reveal, a hint, a time-limited key, a community result. Puzzmo's five-minute head start is a zero-cost perk that rewards subscribing without paying anyone.
- Daily caps and anti-burnout design (Gage) fit a weekly email better than a daily one for a game that already has its own daily loop.

### Gaps
- No measured open or click figures for NYT Gameplay, Puzzmo's newsletter, The Atlantic's games or any crossword newsletter.
- No data found on "hint" emails or leaderboard-by-email for puzzle games.

## Interactive email: AMP, kinetic/CSS-checkbox email and in-email games

### Takeaway
AMP for Email works only in Gmail, Yahoo and Mail.ru, after per-sender allowlisting with SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Outlook.com dropped it in 2020 and Apple Mail never supported it. Its published lift numbers are large but come almost entirely from vendors. CSS "kinetic" email using the checkbox/radio "punched card" hack works in Apple Mail, which is the largest client by opens, but it is hard to make accessible and every interactive element needs a static fallback.

### Cited Findings
- **AMP support:** Gmail, Yahoo Mail, FairEmail and Mail.ru. Not supported in Apple Mail (Litmus representative). — [Bounteous, Apr 2020](https://www.bounteous.com/insights/2020/04/07/amp-email-what-marketers-need-know/)
- **Outlook.com turned AMP off after its developer preview ended on 1 October 2020.** AMP messages fall back to the HTML part. Microsoft pointed senders to Actionable Messages instead. — [Email on Acid](https://www.emailonacid.com/blog/article/industry-news/outlook-turning-off-amp-for-email/); [Spam Resource, Sept 2020](https://www.spamresource.com/2020/09/amp-next-big-thing-not-at-outlookcom.html)
- Google's Gmail AMP developer documentation was still published with a 2025-12-16 update stamp. No announcement that Gmail has ended AMP was found. — [Google Developers](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/ampemail)
- **Registration (amp.dev, official):** emails must pass SPF, DKIM and DMARC with a TLD matching the From: address, include a text/html fallback MIME part, and meet each client's sender requirements. You send a production-ready AMP email to each provider's allowlisting address and fill in one global form. — [amp.dev sender distribution](https://amp.dev/documentation/guides-and-tutorials/start/email_sender_distribution)
- Vendors add that registration is per sender address, not per domain, and takes about 10–15 business days. — [Mailmodo](https://www.mailmodo.com/guides/register-amp-email/); [Mailtarget](https://docs.mailtarget.co/amp-sender-registration-for-interactive-email)
- **Marketer sentiment:** in a 2018 Litmus poll, 31% of marketers who knew AMP said they were very likely to use it. In a later Litmus LinkedIn poll of 333 voters on the trend that's "got to go", AMP for Email came top. — [Litmus blog](https://www.litmus.com/blog/email-trends-amp-for-email)
- **Vendor-reported AMP lifts.** All are from AMP tooling sellers, often on small baselines:
  - Kotak Securities: 360% uplift in conversions and 400% in clicks. — [Netcore](https://netcorecloud.com/?p=101511)
  - MakeMyTrip: 900% CTOR and 600% more responses. — [Stripo statistics roundup](https://stripo.email/blog/amp-emails-fresh-statistics-or-how-companies-earn-with-amp-for-email/)
  - Mailmodo tests: conversion of about 11% for HTML vs 41% for interactive; form submissions of 0.4% for HTML vs 3.7% for AMP. — [Email on Acid](https://www.emailonacid.com/blog/article/industry-news/amp-emails-vs-html/)
- **Kärcher gamified AMP email (roulette wheel with a time-limited promo code), agency-reported:** open rate rose from 27% to 38% and CTR from 2.9% to 6.6%. The agency said gamification works only "if there is a unique selling proposition". — [Promodo case study](https://www.promodo.com/case-studies/how-to-increase-ctr-through-email-gamification); [Yespo](https://yespo.io/blog/karcher-case-study-how-gamification-boosts-ctr-and-open-rate)
- No independent, scratch-card-specific study with lift numbers was found. The widely repeated "48% higher engagement" stat has no traceable primary source. — [search synthesis across Omnisend, Benchmark Email, GetResponse](https://www.benchmarkemail.com/blog/gamified-emails/)
- MailerLite launched a free scratch-card generator for campaigns, which shows the feature has become commodity tooling. — [CreateWith](https://www.createwith.com/tool/mailerlite/updates/mailerlite-launches-free-scratch-card-generator-to-gamify-email-campaigns)
- **Kinetic and CSS-checkbox email:** Mark Robbins (Rebel Mail, acquired by Salesforce) described "punched card coding" in 2015, which uses many radio buttons and `:checked` CSS selectors to hold state. — [WebDesignerDepot, Oct 2015](https://www.webdesignerdepot.com/2015/10/punched-card-coding-the-secret-of-interactive-email/); [Litmus community spotlight](https://www.litmus.com/blog/community-spotlight-mark-robbins)
- Kinetic email is supported by Apple Mail (macOS and iOS), Yahoo and several Android clients. American Express' email design system rates Apple Mail as fully supported but warns that previewers such as Email on Acid cannot preview it properly, so it should be tested on real devices. Stripo found that Apple Mail handled form submissions but iCloud Mail on the web did not. — [FreshInbox, "The Dawn of Kinetic Email"](https://freshinbox.com/blog/the-dawn-of-kinetic-email/); [American Express design system](https://designinternet-qaeusw1green.americanexpress.com/community/marketing-email-design-system/foundations/interactive-kinetic-emails); [Stripo, May 2025](https://stripo.email/blog/smart-fallbacks-for-interactive-emails-how-we-handled-partial-html5-support/)
- Ted Goas listed the open problems with checkbox email: no persistent state, forwarded emails letting someone vote on another person's behalf, and testing tools that only produce static screenshots. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/tedgoas/my-first-interactive-email-94)
- **Accessibility:** with the checkbox hack, a label can't take focus or be activated without a mouse, and screen readers get no context about what changed. Robbins' mitigation uses a hidden real checkbox, a stand-in with `role="button"` and `tabindex=0`, and an `aria-live` visually hidden status. Contributors call the result "a little less inaccessible", not accessible. Recommended fallbacks: stack the content statically, link to a web version, use real headings, test with VoiceOver, NVDA or JAWS, and include a plain-text part. — [Mark Robbins, "A Deep Dive into Email Accessibility"](https://noti.st/markrobbins/bjF6KX/slides); [Stripo](https://stripo.email/blog/accessibility-plus-interactivity-in-one-email-mutually-exclusive-or-just-another-challenge/); [Frontend Masters](https://frontendmasters.com/courses/html-email-v2/checkbox-hack-review); [eMarketing Platform](https://emarketingplatform.com/blog/email-tip-of-the-month-anatomy-of-an-accessible-email/)

### Inferences
- For an iOS-first audience (OutBrick is iOS-first), AMP reaches only the Gmail and Yahoo minority and requires allowlisting. It is a poor fit. CSS-kinetic email reaches Apple Mail, but on the evidence above it cannot be made fully VoiceOver-accessible. For an app with active VoiceOver users, any interactive element should therefore be optional decoration over static content that works on its own: for example, a "tap to reveal the answer" whose fallback simply prints the answer.
- The reliable gamification evidence (Kärcher, Stripo) shows real but moderate gains in clicks. The 360–900% figures come from form-in-email cases on tiny baselines and should not be quoted as expectations.
- Forwarding and lack of state mean in-email polls or votes cannot be trusted as counts. Any vote that matters should land on the website or in the app.

### Gaps
- No current Litmus State of Email figure for AMP or kinetic adoption was retrieved.
- No published lift for kinetic (non-AMP) email in Apple Mail.
- Behaviour of kinetic CSS in the newest Apple Mail versions (iOS 26/27) was not confirmed by a dated source.

## Community email: digests, spotlights, challenges, beta programs

### Takeaway
Forum digests such as Discourse's Activity Summary are designed for *lapsed* members: they go out only after inactivity and stop once someone is clearly gone. Discourse staff call them "extremely important" for retention, but no measured lift was found. Community content in newsletters (NYT's "Community Chatter") is an established pattern.

### Cited Findings
- Discourse's Activity Summary (formerly the digest) goes only to members who haven't visited in a set period. It includes activity statistics, the most popular topics and posts, and topics that are "New for you". It defaults to weekly, and the countdown resets on each visit, so regular members rarely receive one. — [Discourse Meta](https://meta.discourse.org/t/what-are-discourse-digests/36627)
- Discourse staff: summary emails are "extremely important to community engagement and retention". The redesign leads with counts of what you missed and adds "Join the discussion" calls to action. — [Discourse Meta, "Summary Email Changes"](https://meta.discourse.org/t/summary-email-changes/52777)
- One admin shortened the lookback window from 365 to 28 days so that emails stop once a user is clearly not coming back. — [Peyups forum](https://peyups.com/t/peyups-enhancement-proposal-1-enable-activity-summaries-and-invites/3063/10)
- NYT Gameplay's "Community Chatter" section reposts player screenshots and comments. — [The Audiencers](https://theaudiencers.com/wordle-and-beyond-how-games-are-impacting-publisher-newsletters/)

### Inferences
- Digests triggered by inactivity, which go quiet for active users and give up on dead accounts, are a respectful default for a community surface. A "here's what you missed" message that leads with counts is the documented pattern.
- Featuring players' own posts (Community Chatter) costs little and gives social proof, but needs permission or moderation.

### Gaps
- No measured retention lift from Discourse or Reddit digests was found.
- No sourced data on "player of the month", community challenges or beta-tester email programs for mobile games. Not researched in depth because of the budget.

## Referral and advocacy by email

### Takeaway
Morning Brew is the canonical case: milestone rewards that start with *content* (a bonus Sunday issue at 3 referrals), then cheap merchandise, a "Share the Brew" block in every issue, and double opt-in against fraud. Secondhand sources credit referrals with about 30–35% of subscribers. For an iOS app, Apple has in practice rejected rewards given to the *invitee* for downloading or registering, while rewards for the *sender* have been accepted.

### Cited Findings
- **Morning Brew** (secondhand figures, which vary by source):
  - Referrals made up about 30% of growth from 100k to 1.5–1.7M subscribers in 18 months. — [ReferralCandy](https://referralcandy.com/blog/morning-brew-referral-program); [Marketer Milk](https://www.marketermilk.com/links/how-morning-brew-s-referral-program-built-an-audience-of-1-5-million-subscribers)
  - Morning Brew reportedly said in October 2020 that 35% of 2.4M subscribers came from referrals. — [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/newsletter-referral-program)
  - Digiday (2018): 180k subscribers in April 2018 and more than 700k by November; more than 50,000 subscribers had referred at least three people. — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/morning-brew-claims-1-million-subscribers-3m-revenue-profitable-business/)
- **Reward ladder:** 3 referrals unlocks "Light Roast", an extra Sunday newsletter, at no direct cost. Higher tiers bring stickers, a t-shirt and, at the top, a trip to HQ. A "Share the Brew" block in every issue shows the reader's own count and the next reward. — [OMR (German)](https://omr.com/de/daily/morning-brew-referral-marketing-growth-hack); [Referral Rock](https://referralrock.com/blog/morning-brew-referral-program/)
- **Cost:** stickers plus postage came to about $1.25 per referrer, or about $0.25 per referred subscriber, against $4–5 per subscriber on Facebook and Instagram ads. — [The Product Person](https://theproductperson.substack.com/p/-the-product-person-issue-5)
- **Fraud controls:** double opt-in from 2019, with confirmations still above 85%. Tyler Denk also built email validation and a blocklist of more than 600 fake domains and IPs. His advice is to start a referral program only once people already share the product without incentives. — [The Product Person](https://theproductperson.substack.com/p/-the-product-person-issue-5)
- **One-to-one channels:** SMS and WhatsApp shares produced 10x the sign-ups of LinkedIn, 5x Twitter and 2x Facebook (secondhand). — [Growing Viral](https://growingviral.beehiiv.com/p/morning-brew-building-35-million-strong-audience-referrals)
- Beehiiv documents milestone mechanics. Its examples start low (3 referrals → bonus issue). Milk Road put its first reward at a single referral to build momentum. — [beehiiv support](https://www.beehiiv.com/support/article/13090888705943-Referral-Program-Getting-set-up-sharing-and-monitoring); [beehiiv blog](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/newsletter-referral-program)
- **Duolingo:** third-party guides say referrers get about one week of Super per friend, capped at about ten weeks, but they disagree on the trigger and on whether the invitee gets anything. No official terms or performance data were found. — [GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/examples/language-learning-referral-programs/)
- **Apple App Store:** Guideline 3.2.2 (2017 wording) says apps should not require users to rate, review, watch videos, download other apps or tap ads to access functionality or receive compensation. A developer reported a 2021 rejection whose reviewer note said rewarding the *sender* is acceptable but the *invitee* "should not receive any rewards for downloading or registering". That note is a rejection message, not published policy. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 674832](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/674832); [TouchArcade, June 2017](https://toucharcade.com/2017/06/09/did-apple-just-kill-incentivized-video-ads-again-nobodys-sure/)
- Apple: developers who manipulate reviews or chart ranking with fake or paid reviews or other inappropriate methods may be removed from the program. Google Play prohibits incentives in exchange for ratings. — [Apple Developer Forums thread 108548](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/108548); [summary via Newly guide, July 2026](https://newly.app/guides/app-referral-program)

### Inferences
- For a free game with no cash rewards, Morning Brew's first tier is the most transferable part: a *content* reward such as a bonus puzzle, a cosmetic or an early-access board, which costs nothing and filters for loyal readers.
- Under App Review practice, an in-app referral should reward only the sender, after a real action. It should never gate core play behind invites and never reward a rating. A newsletter referral that rewards someone for signing up to the *email list*, rather than downloading the app, is outside App Store review. That is an inference, not confirmed policy.
- Double opt-in plus counting only confirmed referrals is the minimum defence against fraud on a free list.
- Showing each reader their own referral count and next milestone inside every issue (Share the Brew) is the documented mechanic, rather than a one-off campaign.

### Gaps
- No primary Morning Brew or Denk post was fetched. All Morning Brew numbers are secondhand and conflict slightly (1.5M vs 1.7M; 30% vs 35%).
- No data found on Dropbox's referral results (the well-known "+500 MB" figures predate 2019 and were not re-sourced), on Spotify or Duolingo family-invite performance, or on referral programs in free mobile puzzle games.
- The current wording of Apple guideline 3.2.2(x) was not verified on Apple's live page.

## Behind-the-scenes and devlog emails from indie studios

### Takeaway
The best-measured indie example is Failbetter Games (Fallen London). One personal, plain-text, single-ask email to a list of hundreds of thousands got 26% opens, 2% clicks, 0.6% unsubscribes and about 1,200 Steam wishlists, roughly what a major Steam event delivers. Practitioners stress a personal voice, reintroducing yourself and one call to action. No measured data was found for Stardew Valley, Team Cherry, Monument Valley, Alto's or Inkle email programs.

### Cited Findings
- **Failbetter Games, June 2022:** Fallen London has "well over a million accounts" but only "hundreds of thousands" opted into marketing. One email for Mask of the Rose, written by communications director Hannah Flynn, got a 26% open rate, 2% CTR, 0.6% unsubscribes and about 1,200 wishlists with a long tail. Flynn said that matched a major Steam event. The author's benchmarks: typical opens of 15–30%, CTR of 1–15%, and about 3% unsubscribes as a spam-flag danger zone. — [How To Market A Game (Chris Zukowski), 28 June 2022](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/06/28/using-mailing-lists-to-gain-thousands-of-wishlists/)
- **Failbetter's craft advice:** plain, mostly text layouts that don't feel corporate; reintroduce yourself ("Wait – who are you?"); write to "you" one-to-one; use the house voice ("Hello, delicious friend") and in-universe story; one call to action; don't fear unsubscribes; build the list across games so fans follow the studio. — [How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/06/28/using-mailing-lists-to-gain-thousands-of-wishlists/)
- Practitioner advice from a vendor blog, not measured: start devlogs 12–18 months before launch; someone who has watched five devlog episodes is "already invested"; don't bombard wishlisters with daily updates. — [Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/blog/game-dev-devlog-best-practices-marketing-community)
- itch.io has historically not let developers email followers directly. — [itch.io forum](https://itch.io/t/148037/how-do-followers-get-notified-of-a-game-being-finished-with-development-do-followers-also-get-notified-of-devlogs)

### Inferences
- Failbetter's numbers are a realistic anchor for a studio emailing its existing players about something new: about 26% opens (before MPP effects were widespread), about 2% clicks, under 1% unsubscribes. A personal letter with one ask beat a polished template, according to its author.
- The list is most valuable as a cross-title or cross-feature asset. Failbetter's advice to grow it across games applies equally to a studio's next big feature or event.

### Gaps
- No sourced email data or newsletter descriptions were found for Stardew Valley (ConcernedApe), Team Cherry, ustwo (Monument Valley), Snowman (Alto's) or Inkle. These studios appear to communicate mainly through blogs and social posts, but that is unverified.
- No Game Developer (gamedeveloper.com) article with measured newsletter impact was found in this pass.
